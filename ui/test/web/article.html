The Quiet Return of the Night Train

By Jane Doe · October 12, 2026 · 8 min read

Across Europe, sleeper services that were written off a decade ago are filling up again. Rail operators say demand has tripled since 2021, and new routes from Vienna, Brussels and Zürich are selling out weeks ahead.

The reasons are partly practical — a night in a couchette replaces both a flight and a hotel — and partly something harder to name. “People want the journey back,” says one conductor, who has worked the Vienna–Rome line for nineteen years.

A network rebuilt from spare parts

Most of the rolling stock is second-hand. Carriages retired in the 1990s have been stripped, rewired and fitted with USB sockets, a detail passengers mention more often than the new mattresses. The config file for the booking system, routes.yaml, now lists 42 overnight services.

We thought aviation had won. It turns out people just needed a reason to choose the slower way.

What changed

  • Cheaper track access charges on several international corridors.
  • Climate-conscious travellers, especially under 30, booking as a group.
    • Six-berth couchettes sell first.
    • Private cabins sell last but earn the most.
  • Better booking apps that show the whole route at once.

How to book one

  1. Pick a route and a date at least three weeks out.
  2. Choose a seat, a couchette or a sleeper cabin.
  3. Bring earplugs. Seriously.

Operators expose a small API for timetables. A request looks like this:

curl https://api.example.org/v1/trains?from=VIE&to=ROM \
  -H 'Accept: application/json'
{ "train": "NJ 233", "departs": "19:23", "berths": 4 }
